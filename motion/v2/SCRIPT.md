# THREAD — Keep going.

Revision 2. A 43-second film built from requests and useful results. Landscape
and portrait are both composed at 60 fps. There is no slow introductory monologue.
The first request begins almost immediately and a real-shaped result appears in
the first second. The conversation drives the cut.

## Motion language

Large useful objects, fewer labels. A route becomes a destination; a destination
becomes weather; an itinerary becomes a saved object. Each result enters with
continuous eased motion and a short masked reveal; text from adjacent shots
never shares the same pixels. No dead fade-to-black
between tasks. No numbered chapter labels, diagnostic rows, microcopy headers or
background wire-wave wallpaper. The large-scale hierarchy is request → result.

Dark ink and electric blue remain the identity, with pale paper tickets and
photographic moments for visual contrast. Airline marks belong inside the result.
Cards contain information; they are not decorative empty boxes.

## Sound direction

Brisk, natural character delivery. Keep the same vocal identity throughout.
Record the user lines as one performance with separable gaps. The assistant's
answer is short. The music is an original 120 BPM rhythmic electronic score,
with tight percussion, a moving bassline, useful UI sounds and short transitions.
The interruption makes a quick rhythmic cut, then immediately moves forward.

## Production truth

The film uses cinematic representations of implemented capabilities, rather
than claiming every composited panel is an unedited screen capture. The flight
shortlist is still a demonstration; the result includes **Sample fares** in
normal readable type. Airline marks identify the sample visual and imply no
partnership, booking, live fare feed or transaction. Actual sports source data
is retained. Research sources are identified. Weather and clocks are dated
captures. Itineraries and lists are AI draft examples. The music and illustrative
photos are original production assets. No Spotify playback, offline AI,
always-on wake word, deployment detail or “no laptop” claim appears in the cut.

Covered families: continuous voice, interruption, retained context, flight
shortlists, weather, itineraries, checklists, save/recall, sports and format
filters, Google search and tab correction, research, calculation, timers, world
clocks, and home-screen widgets. The feature backlog is not represented as shipped.
